# Year 3 — WA Curriculum summary

## English

**Language**
- Texts have purposes (inform, persuade, entertain) and typical structures; paragraphs group ideas.
- Sentences: a sentence has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete idea; simple and compound sentences (*and, but, so, or*); beginning complex sentences (*because, when*).
- Word classes: nouns (common/proper), verbs (action, saying, thinking), adjectives, adverbs; noun groups; verb tense (past/present/future).
- Punctuation: capital letters, full stops, question and exclamation marks; commas in lists; apostrophes for contractions (and simple possession); quotation marks for direct speech (introduced).
- Spelling: letter–sound patterns incl. vowel digraphs/trigraphs (*ai, ay, ee, ea, igh, oa, ow, ou, oi, oy*); consonant blends; common suffixes (*-s, -es, -ed, -ing, -er, -est, -ful, -ly*); compound words; homophones (*there/their, to/too/two, hear/here*); high-frequency words.
- Vocabulary: topic words, words with similar/opposite meanings, words with more than one meaning.

**Literacy (reading & comprehension)**
- Read texts with fluency; self-monitor and fix up (reread, read on, use pictures).
- Comprehension strategies: activate prior knowledge, **predict**, **visualise**, **question**, **clarify** unknown words, **sequence**, find **main idea and details**, **summarise**, make **inferences** (literal vs inferred meaning).
- Use text features: title, headings, pictures, captions, diagrams, maps, labels, glossary, contents.
- Write short informative, persuasive and imaginative texts; plan, draft, re-read and edit.
- Speaking & listening: retell, ask and answer questions, share opinions with reasons.

**Literature**
- Characters, settings and events; how authors use language to describe; poems (rhyme, rhythm, repetition); connect stories to own experience.

## Science (content source for English texts)
- **Biological:** living things can be grouped on observable features; living vs non-living.
- **Chemical:** a change of state between solid and liquid can be caused by adding or removing heat.
- **Earth & Space:** Earth's rotation on its axis causes regular changes, including day and night.
- **Physical:** heat can be produced in many ways and can move from one object to another.
- **Science as a Human Endeavour:** science involves making predictions and describing patterns.

## HASS (content source for English texts)
- **Civics & Citizenship:** why we make decisions democratically (voting); who makes rules; how people contribute to their community.
- **Economics & Business:** needs vs wants; making choices because resources are limited.
- **Geography:** Australia's states, territories and capital cities; Australia's neighbouring countries; climates and seasons; types of settlement (city, town, rural, remote); Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Country/Place.
- **History:** Country/Place of the local Aboriginal people (Whadjuk Noongar in Perth); how the local community has changed; significant days and celebrations (e.g. ANZAC Day, Remembrance Day, NAIDOC Week, Australia Day, Harmony Week) and why they matter.

## Foundation knowledge to check (common confusions at this age)
Use these as quick checks in warm-ups; fix misconceptions explicitly in texts.
- The Earth spins (day/night) and travels around the Sun (year); the Sun does not move around the Earth.
- Australia is a country *and* a continent; Perth is a city in the state of Western Australia.
- Seasons in Australia are opposite to the northern hemisphere (Christmas is in summer here).
- Living things grow, need food/water/air, reproduce; fire and cars "move" but are not alive.
- Rules (home/school/club) vs laws (made by parliaments, apply to everyone).
- Past, present and future; a timeline reads left to right from earlier to later.
