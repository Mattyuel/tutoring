## Teacher notes
- **Common misconceptions:**
  - **Reading = as "write the answer"** (the operational view of the equals sign). This is the big one this week. Signs: answering 8 + 7 = □ + 4 with 15; marking 15 = 9 + 6 as false; "running" working such as 9 + 5 = 14 + 6 = 20 (Q6). Fix it by saying "is the same as" aloud for every =, drawing a balance, and asking "what is each side worth?" Keep insisting on correct use of = in their own working from now on.
  - Using the wrong inverse when the unknown is **subtracted or the divisor** (125 − □ = 67 → 192; 84 ÷ □ = 7 → 588). Ask them to check by substituting, and use part-part-whole: "125 is the whole; 67 is one part; what's the other part?"
  - Treating subtraction like addition when making equivalent sentences (63 − 18 = 65 − 16 is false). F12 investigates this directly.
  - Two-step unknowns: undoing in the **wrong order** (for 3 × □ + 4 = 25 doing 25 ÷ 3 first). Use the "socks and shoes" image: what was done last is undone first.
  - Perimeter with □: forgetting a □ appears twice (rectangle, house shape) or counting only the labelled sides.
- **If the student finishes early:** F11 and F12 are rich. Extend F11 with three shapes and three sentences; extend F12 by asking whether the rules work for fractions (½ + ¾ = 1 + □). Invite the student to design their own shape-value grid (Q20) with a unique solution and swap it with you.
- **If the student struggles:** use a real or drawn balance with counters or cubes; cover the unknown with a sticky note and ask "what number is hiding?"; use fact-family triangles; act out function machines with a partner ("you are the × 4 machine").
- **Sources / fact-check:** In Australian Rules football a goal is worth 6 points and a behind 1 point (AFL Laws of the Game). The football scores, ticket prices, bus timetable, ribbon, marbles, stickers and excursion numbers are invented for the problems and labelled as made up or hypothetical where they could be mistaken for real data.
- **Next week builds on (Week 33 — timetables and 24-hour time):** X4's "work backwards from an arrival time" becomes planning a Transperth-style trip from a timetable; inverse operations with durations (□ + 35 min = 9:15 am) and 24-hour time conversions (Week 9 and Warm-up f) are the core skills. Revise am/pm ↔ 24-hour time before the session if Warm-up f was a slip.
