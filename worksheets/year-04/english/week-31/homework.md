## 1 · Read

> ### Finding North at Sea
>
> Before compasses, sailors found their way by carefully watching the Sun by day and the stars at night. On cloudy days and stormy nights, however, the sky could not help them, and ships could easily drift off course.
>
> A magnetic compass changed this. Its needle is a small magnet that turns until one end points roughly north, so sailors could check their direction at any time, in almost any weather. Once they knew where north was, they could work out east, south and west too.
>
> A ship's compass had to be looked after carefully. Big pieces of iron or steel nearby can pull the needle off course, so the compass was kept in a special stand, called a **binnacle**, near the ship's wheel. Many ship compasses float in liquid, which steadies the needle as the ship rolls on the waves.
>
> Today, most ships also use satellite navigation. Even so, sailors still carry a compass, because it needs no batteries and keeps working when electronic equipment fails.
