## 1 · Read

> ### Nature's Window
>
> In Kalbarri National Park, about six hundred kilometres north of Perth, the Murchison River has cut a deep, winding gorge through layers of red and white sandstone. This sandstone formed from sand that was laid down in flat layers hundreds of millions of years ago, long before there were any dinosaurs.
>
> High above the river sits one of the park's most famous sights: Nature's Window, a natural arch of layered rock. Visitors climb up to it and look through the gap to see the river bending far below, framed like a picture.
>
> How did the window form? Some layers of sandstone are softer than others. Over a very long time, wind and rain weathered the weaker layers, and the loose grains were eroded away. The harder layers above were left standing, creating a hole right through the rock.
>
> The river has been doing the same kind of work on a much larger scale. Each time it floods, the fast-moving water carries sand and pebbles that scrape the bottom and sides of the gorge, making it slightly deeper and wider.
