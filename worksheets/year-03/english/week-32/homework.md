## 3 · Read a new text (8 min)

> ### Keeping Warm, Keeping Cool
>
> Animals cannot put on a jumper or turn on a fan, but they still need to stay at the right temperature. Many animals have clever ways to do it.
>
> **Keeping warm.** Mammals and birds make their own body heat, just like you. Fur and feathers trap air close to the skin. Trapped air is an insulator, so it slows down the heat leaving the animal's body. On a cold morning, a bird fluffs up its feathers to trap even more air. Whales and seals have a thick layer of fat under their skin, called **blubber**. Blubber is an insulator, too.
>
> **Keeping cool.** On hot days, animals need to lose heat. Dogs pant with their tongues out. Kangaroos lick their front legs. As the spit dries, it carries heat away from their skin. Koalas have a clever trick: on very hot days they hug the trunks of trees. The trunk is cooler than the air, so heat moves from the koala's warm body into the tree.
>
> **Borrowing heat.** Lizards, like the bobtails in Perth gardens, cannot make enough heat inside their bodies. On a cool morning, a bobtail lies in the sunshine to warm up before it goes looking for food.
