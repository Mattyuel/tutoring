### Part H · Writing — model answer (≈105 words)
*The Sun does not really go anywhere at night. The Earth is a giant ball that spins on its axis, and one spin takes about 24 hours. When our side of the Earth faces the Sun, it is day. When the Earth turns and our side faces away from the Sun, it is night. In the morning we turn back into the sunlight, so we see a sunrise. The Sun looks like it moves across the sky because we are spinning, just like the world seems to slide past when you ride a merry-go-round. So the Sun stays still, and we move!*

**Marking notes:** opening answers the question · correct explanation of day **and** night · ≥4 word-bank words · a *because*/*so* reason · sentence punctuation · makes sense when re-read. Pick **one** spelling to correct together rather than marking every error. The diagram should show the Sun, the Earth, the lit/dark halves and a spin arrow, with labels.

## Teacher notes
- **Why this topic:** concrete, demonstrable with an orange and a torch, and it fixes a very common confusion (*"the Sun goes down"*). It also separates two ideas children often mix up: **spin = day**, **orbit = year**.
- **Misconceptions to listen for:** the Sun moves around the Earth; the Sun hides behind hills or "goes to sleep"; the Moon or clouds make night; it is night everywhere at once; the Earth is flat; the Moon makes its own light.
- **Language note:** we still say "the Sun rises" — tell her this describes how it **looks** from Earth.
- **If she struggles:** act it out — she is the Earth, a lamp is the Sun; she turns slowly on the spot and says "sunrise… day… sunset… night". Reduce the Keep going section and spend more time on Parts C–E.
- **If she flies through it:** K3, K4 and K7; or ask *"If the Earth is spinning so fast, why don't we fly off?"* (preview of gravity).
- **Accuracy notes:** Earth's diameter ≈ 12 742 km (driving through at 100 km/h ≈ 127 h ≈ 5.3 days); ground speed at the equator ≈ 1 670 km/h (Perth, at about 32° S, ≈ 1 400 km/h); airliner cruise ≈ 900 km/h; Sun ≈ 150 million km away, sunlight takes ≈ 8 min 20 s; the Sun's volume is ≈ 1.3 million Earths; Earth rotates once per ≈ 24 h (solar day) and orbits in ≈ 365¼ days; Copernicus (born 1473 in Toruń, Poland) published *De revolutionibus* in 1543 — "nearly 500 years ago"; Sydney is 2 h (3 h in daylight saving) ahead of Perth; when it is noon in Perth it is about 4–5 am in England. Possums, many bats (microbats echolocate), owls, wombats and tawny frogmouths are nocturnal; tawny frogmouths camouflage as branches by day.
