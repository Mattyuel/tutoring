## Teacher notes
- **Common misconceptions:** $-3^2$ vs $(-3)^2$; doing ÷/× strictly in the order "D then M" rather than left to right; thinking 1 is prime; LCM = product always.
- **If the student finishes early:** ask them to *prove* HCF × LCM = product for two numbers using the "lowest power / highest power" rule (each prime's two indices get used exactly once between the HCF and LCM).
- **If the student struggles:** do factor trees side-by-side and use a Venn diagram of prime factors for HCF/LCM (shared primes in the overlap = HCF; whole diagram = LCM).
- **Facts:** bus frequencies and the Antarctic temperatures are hypothetical (the question says "suppose").
- **Next week builds on:** index notation → index laws; prime factorisation is reused for simplifying surds-style square roots and estimating roots.
