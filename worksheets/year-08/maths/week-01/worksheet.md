## Part B · Learn ⏱ ~8 min

### 1. Integer rules
| Operation | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Adding a negative | same as subtracting | $5 + (-8) = 5 - 8 = -3$ |
| Subtracting a negative | same as adding | $-4 - (-9) = -4 + 9 = 5$ |
| × or ÷, **same** signs | positive | $(-6)\times(-7) = 42$ |
| × or ÷, **different** signs | negative | $-56 \div 8 = -7$ |

**Watch out:** $(-3)^2 = (-3)\times(-3) = 9$, but $-3^2 = -(3\times3) = -9$. The index applies only to what it is touching.

### 2. Order of operations
**B**rackets → **I**ndices (powers & roots) → **D**ivision & **M**ultiplication (left to right) → **A**ddition & **S**ubtraction (left to right).

> **Worked example 1:** $-3 \times (-4) - 18 \div (-6)$
> $= 12 - (-3)$  *(do × and ÷ first)*
> $= 12 + 3 = 15$

### 3. Prime factorisation and index form
A **prime** has exactly two factors: 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …). **1 is not prime.**
Every whole number greater than 1 can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (the *Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic*).

> **Worked example 2:** $360 = 36 \times 10 = (2^2 \times 3^2) \times (2 \times 5) = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$

### 4. HCF and LCM from prime factors
- **HCF** (highest common factor): take the primes the numbers **share**, each to the **lowest** power.
- **LCM** (lowest common multiple): take **every** prime that appears, each to the **highest** power.

> **Worked example 3:** $84 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $120 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5$
> HCF $= 2^2 \times 3 = 12$  LCM $= 2^3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 = 840$
> *Check:* HCF × LCM $= 12 \times 840 = 10\,080 = 84 \times 120$ ✔ (true for any two numbers — can you see why?)

### 5. Squares, cubes and roots
A number is a **perfect square** when every index in its prime factorisation is even, and a **perfect cube** when every index is a multiple of 3.
$\sqrt{2^4 \times 3^2} = 2^2 \times 3 = 12$ (halve the indices)  $\sqrt[3]{2^6 \times 5^3} = 2^2 \times 5 = 20$ (divide the indices by 3)
