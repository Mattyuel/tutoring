## Teacher notes
- **Common misconceptions:**
  - Sign slips with negatives: $4 - (-2) = 2$ (Q4 Mia). Encourage counting the run and rise on a sketch as a check.
  - Adding run and rise instead of using Pythagoras (Q4 Sam) — link back to "the hypotenuse is shorter than the two legs together".
  - Mixing up the formulas: distance **subtracts**, midpoint **adds** (Q4 Ava).
  - Forgetting the second solution of $(k - 1)^2 = 25$ (X3, HW3). Ask "where else could the point be?"
  - Rounding intermediate values (Q13 b, Q10 b); keep $AC^2 = 45$ and $k = \tfrac{65}{\sqrt{337}}$ in the calculator.
  - Adding unlike surds: $\sqrt{45} + \sqrt{20} \ne \sqrt{65}$; simplify first, then collect only like surds.
  - Rounding **down** a minimum size (Q14 b) — a 57.7 cm box is too small.
- **If the student finishes early:** F9 (spider and fly) and F12 are the richest; extension — find the *perpendicular bisector* of $AB$ in F6 as an equation $y = mx + c$ (Week 28), or explore which point on the line $y = x$ is closest to $(4, 0)$.
- **If the student struggles:** always draw the run/rise triangle on grid paper before using the formula; use only positive coordinates first; restrict Part D to Q9 a), Q10 a) and Q12 a) (all whole-number triples).
- **Calculator use:** Parts A–C non-calculator except 🖩 items; Part D calculator. Exact answers expected wherever no rounding is asked for.
- **Facts and contexts:**
  - 1 inch = 2.54 cm **exactly** (international inch, defined in 1959).
  - TV "screen size" is the diagonal of the viewable screen in inches; 16 : 9 is the standard widescreen (HDTV) aspect ratio and 4 : 3 the older standard. Real sets vary slightly, so Q10 is about the screen only.
  - The spider and fly puzzle (F9) is a classic by the English puzzlist Henry Dudeney (published in the early 1900s, collected in *The Canterbury Puzzles*, 1907). Answer of 40 checked by unfolding (see F9).
  - Roof, kite, ferry, jet ski, walking trail, boat ramp, shed and car-boot measurements and prices are invented (labelled hypothetical or "suppose" in the questions). The car boot is described as "roughly a box".
- **Next week (Week 33: scientific notation & significant figures) builds on:** rounding to decimal places in this week's 🖩 answers → rounding to significant figures; index laws (Week 2, Part A Q4) → powers of 10; very large and small distances (e.g. distance formula with coordinates in thousands of km or micrometres) make a natural bridge. A good opener: "Round $\sqrt{337}$ to 3 significant figures."
